\subsection{Initial ideas}
\begin{reviewblock}

\textbf{Related work:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Human teams: cooperation vs competition (Deutsch 1949), equality vs equity in reward splitting (Deutsch 1975), social loafing (Karau \& Williams 1993)
    \item Humans with computers and robots: ``Can computers be teammates?'' (Nass et al.\ 1996), in-group robots (Kuchenbrandt et al.\ 2013; Fraune et al.\ 2017)
    \item Human Performance in Competitive and Collaborative Human–Machine Teams(2025)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Other design options:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The robot presented as a different robot each round, with a new name and colour
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Game Changes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Change colour depending on who would score points
    \item Robot AI
    \item need robot agent to be believeable (e.g. robot sprite).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Questionnaire Questions}
Pre-Game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Atari before
    \item Robot experiments before
    \item Maybe declare age/gender
\end{itemize}

Post-Game:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How helpful is robot (Peer review)
    \item How do you think of your performance?
    \item {For shared score players:} How well do you think the robot did compared to you? (score distribution)
    \item {For split score players:} Did seeing the robot's score make you more competitive?
    \item How likeable is the robot.
\end{itemize}

Data to collect:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Initial settings (ie shared score, robot skill)
    \item Actual scores
    \item Survey answers
    \item Duration of rounds
\end{itemize}

\end{reviewblock}

\subsection{Research question}
Are people more likely to treat a robot partner as part of their team when the score is shared rather than split, and does the robot's performance affect whether they treat it as a teammate?

\subsection{Hypotheses}

\paragraph{H1:} Participants will perceive the robots at a given performance level more like a teammate if the score is shared compared with if the score is split.

\paragraph{H2:} Participants will perceive a robot at a higher performance level as a teammate compared to a robot at a lower performance level, regardless of whether the score is shared or split.
