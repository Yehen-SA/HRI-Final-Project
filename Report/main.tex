\section{Research Question and Hypotheses}

\subsection{Research question}
Does showing a shared score, rather than separate scores, make people see a robot partner as a teammate, and does this hold regardless of how well the robot performs?

\subsection{Related work}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textit{Deutsch (1949)}: people who share a goal cooperate rather than compete; a shared score gives the participant and robot a shared goal (H1).
    \item \textit{Nass, Fogg \& Moon (1996)}: people treated a computer as a teammate when they shared its goal and outcome (H1).
    \item \textit{Bennett et al.\ (2025)}: team benefits were weaker with a machine partner in an arcade game, so we test whether the effect survives a poorly performing robot (H2).
\end{itemize}
None of these studies varies how the score is displayed when playing with a robot.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbf{H1:}] Participants who see a shared score will see the robot as more of a teammate than those who see split scores.
    
    \item[\textbf{H2:}] The effect in H1 will hold at both robot performance levels.     
\end{itemize}
