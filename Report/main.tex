\section{Research Question and Hypotheses}
\spec{Choice of Question and Hypothesis (15 pts)}

\subsection{Initial ideas}
\textit{Chosen direction:} shared vs split score, with the robot's performance varied:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Score structure: shared score vs split (per-player) score
    \item Robot performance: worse than, matching, or better than the participant
    \item Blame dropped to keep the study simple (possible future work)
\end{itemize}

\textit{Possible games:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Overcooked (Given): already built, strong teamwork; needs a working robot agent
    \item Team quiz: player answers each question or passes it to the robot; simplest to build
    \item Co-op Breakout: two paddles defend shared lives; bricks credited to the last paddle to hit the ball
    \item Co-op memory pairs: turn-based card matching; weaker teamwork
    \item Collecting game: both players collect items on a grid; risks feeling like a race
\end{itemize}
\textit{Game requirements} (rules out Pico Park, Fireboy + Watergirl, bomb defuse):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Playable solo by one participant alongside the robot
    \item Tasks that can be delegated between player and robot
    \item Score that can easily be shown as shared or split per player
    \item Robot's performance can be controlled (worse / matching / better)
    \item Player and robot depend on each other, so the game doesn't become a race
\end{itemize}

\textit{Initial thoughts on what to measure (dependent variables):}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Whether the participant sees the robot as a teammate, competitor or tool
    \item Reward allocation: split bonus points between self and robot
    \item Peer review of the robot as a team member
    \item Participant's guess of how much the robot contributed (vs actual)
    \item Likeability of the robot
\end{itemize}

\textit{Related work to look at:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Human teams: cooperation vs competition (Deutsch 1949), equality vs equity in reward splitting (Deutsch 1975), social loafing (Karau \& Williams 1993)
    \item Humans with computers and robots: ``Can computers be teammates?'' (Nass et al.\ 1996), in-group robots (Kuchenbrandt et al.\ 2013; Fraune et al.\ 2017)
\end{itemize}

\textit{Other ideas / open questions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Score between subjects, performance within subjects \fix{confirm with group}
    \item Robot presented as a different robot each round (name and colour)
    \item How to control the robot's performance, e.g.\ adapting to the participant's score
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research question}
Are people more likely to treat a robot partner as part of their team when the score is shared rather than split, and does this depend on how well the robot performs relative to them?

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1:} A shared score makes participants see the robot more as a teammate.
    \item \textbf{H2:} With a shared score, rewards are split equally; with a split score, they follow the robot's actual contribution.
    \item \textbf{H3:} With a split score, a robot that outscores the participant is seen more as a competitor.
    \item \textbf{H4:} With a shared score, participants underestimate the robot's contribution.
\end{itemize}
